Bottle cooler repairs

How we approach it

Bottle coolers work hard: doors opened all day, packed shelves, often in a hot shopfront or under a canopy. Dust on the condenser and a tired fan motor are the most common reasons they stop cooling, and both are quick fixes when caught early.

We repair branded and unbranded coolers of all makes. If the cooler belongs to a drinks supplier, check your agreement first, then call us if they've left it to you.

Signs you need us

  • Drinks cool at the bottom but warm at the top
  • Cooler runs all the time but never gets cold
  • Ice building up on the back panel
  • Loud buzzing, rattling or a fan that has stopped
  • Condensation running down the inside of the glass
  • Lights work but the compressor won't start

What we repair

  • Condenser cleaning and fan motor replacement
  • Thermostat and temperature control repairs
  • Gas leak repairs and regas
  • Compressor start relays and overloads
  • Door gaskets and self-closing door hinges
  • LED lighting and wiring faults

Questions

Bottle cooler repairs: common questions

Is it worth repairing an old bottle cooler?

Often, yes. Most faults are a fan motor, thermostat, start relay or a gas leak, all far cheaper than a new cooler. If the compressor has failed on an old unit, we'll tell you honestly whether replacing it makes sense.

How often should a bottle cooler's condenser be cleaned?

In a dusty shop or near a road, every three months. Clogged condensers are the number one reason coolers stop cooling in summer.

Can you repair coolers that belong to a drinks company?

Technically yes, but many supplier-owned coolers must be repaired by the supplier. Check your agreement first so you don't pay for something they cover.
