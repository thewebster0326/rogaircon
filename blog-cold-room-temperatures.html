What Temperature Should a Cold Room Be? Meat, Dairy and Veg Guide

The right temperature depends on what you store. Here are typical targets, and how to make sure your cold room is actually holding them.

Typical target temperatures

These are common working ranges. Always follow the supplier's instructions and any food-safety rules that apply to your business.

  • Fresh meat and poultry: about 0 to 4 degrees Celsius
  • Dairy products: about 1 to 4 degrees Celsius
  • Most vegetables: about 2 to 7 degrees Celsius
  • Many fruits and some vegetables (such as bananas and tomatoes) prefer warmer, around 8 to 13 degrees Celsius
  • Frozen foods: minus 18 degrees Celsius or colder

Mixed storage needs compromise

Many businesses store everything in one cold room. In that case, meat and dairy set the limit, so the room runs at the colder end and chill-sensitive produce goes to the warmest spot near the door, or into a separate fridge.

Don't trust the display alone

The controller shows the temperature where its probe is, usually near the evaporator, which is the coldest spot. Product at the back or near the door can be warmer. Check with a probe thermometer in the product itself at different spots and times of day, especially after deliveries.

Signs your cold room is struggling

  • Takes hours to pull down after a delivery
  • Temperature rises during busy trading hours
  • Ice on the evaporator coil
  • Condensing unit running non-stop
